**What it means.** Shrinking the deduction does not rescue it: trailing Full minus rec_hat still loses to trailing Full in every season. The regression shows why: a trailing reception is worth more future Standard points than a trailing Standard point is (coefficient on trailing receptions about 0.3 per game for RB, 0.7-0.8 for WR and TE, 2-3x the Standard coefficient for WR/TE), so receptions are usage, and a trailing rate with them removed is a worse forecast. That is a statement about trailing rates, not about converting a forecast. Once the anchor is a calibrated Full forecast (vi), the conversion no longer loses: subtracting rec_hat is neutral to slightly positive, and dividing by (1 + share_hat) from the share model wins in 11 of 11 seasons (primary). So applying a reception deduction to a Full-anchored forecast is sound provided the anchor is a forecast that already prices usage (a chart is) and the deduction is the shrunk share, not raw history.

## Caveats

- Hyperparameter leakage: train seasons after the test season use the test season's games as history features (not targets). Only 3 scalars per position are tuned, and the folds pick the same values.
- 2015 decision points have no prior seasons in the file; histories start in 2015.
- A week with no row is treated as not played (bye, injury, or no stat line).
- MSE is unweighted across rows; ROS truth over 3+ games is itself noisy, which caps all Spearmans.
- `P_forecast` uses the recency-weighted history Standard ppg as the denominator, noisier than a chart's value-implied points; the realistic slope for a chart sits between the two D variants.
- F uses 2-3 seasons and 28-85 players per position; 2026 is partial.
