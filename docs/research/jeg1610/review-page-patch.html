Version 3: unwinding DDF's own values

Your prescription of 2026-11-10 replaced the earlier one. We applied it only to FantasyPros (assumed Half) and FantasyCalc (its Full list, assumed). CBS Sports and USA Today are used as published. The tests were committed before any code.

  1. Third-party trade chartsEach chart's saved list at its anchor scoring.
  2. The value that feeds DDFThat value is the chart's Adjusted value. Indexed is display only: it is the published list times one number, so unwinding it just gives the list back.
  3. Unwind to value above waiversWithin a position, Adjusted rises with the chart's value, so it can be inverted exactly for every player above the chart's waiver line. The position weights and the pie are the same for every source, so they cancel in step 4. Two things are lost. Players at or below waivers have an Adjusted value of 0, and we only know they sit at or below the line (128 FantasyCalc and 160 FantasyPros player-weeks). The chart's own view of position levels is also lost, because every source's Adjusted total at a position equals the pie times DDF's weight. That view survives only as one of seven votes in the weights.
  4. Expected pointsFor each player, the points per game at which DDF prices a projection at the same Adjusted value, from the same run.
  5. How he earns his pointsReceptions per game and other points per game over his 2024 to 2026 games, from weekly actuals (at least 6 games). 91 to 95% of priced players have their own history. Rookies and players with fewer games use their position's median mix, labelled (12 to 15 players in each scored set).
  6. Implied statsHis implied points split in his own proportions: receptions, and yards and touchdowns by category.
  7. Rebuild each scoring and averageRescore the implied stats, convert back to the chart's units, run DDF unchanged at that scoring, and average the four charts as DDF does. Games do not enter: DDF prices per game, so ESPN's games count has nothing to change.

Why it failed

The same reason as versions 1 and 2. Giving each player the full change in what a catch is worth moves the order much more than the publishers' own columns do. Swapping each player's history for the projections' mix changes nothing (1 of 60 cell-weeks). The same is true if we re-derive the waiver line at the new scoring instead of using the chart's curve (0 of 40). Converting back through the chart's curve at the anchor also wrecks levels. At Standard, most tight ends fall below the anchor's waiver line and go to 0.

Extreme players

Pass-catching backs move up more than other backs, as at CBS Sports and USA Today, but 3 to 4 times too far (FantasyCalc +0.67 against about +0.16). Tight ends all move far too much, whatever their mix. Rushing quarterbacks keep their chart value, which is right. But DDF v3 still lowers them from Standard to Full almost 3 times as much as current DDF, because every other position's rebuilt values grow.

Keep current DDF Value. Nothing live changes. Say in the article that the charts' own format columns move far less than any stat rebuild predicts. Recommended.
